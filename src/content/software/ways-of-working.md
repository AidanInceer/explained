Teams need some shared way to decide what to work on, see progress and improve. **Agile** is the family of ideas behind most of today's processes, and **Scrum** and **Kanban** are the two most common forms. Each comes with rituals: stand-ups, sprints, boards, retrospectives. Knowing what each ritual is *for* lets you keep the useful part and drop the theatre.

## The agile idea

In 2001, a group of developers wrote the **Agile Manifesto**. Its core is short: deliver working software in small pieces, often; work closely with the people who will use it; and change the plan as you learn, rather than following a large plan made at the start. Most of what follows is a way to put that into practice.

## Scrum

Scrum organises work into **sprints**: fixed periods, usually two weeks.

- **Sprint planning**: the team chooses work for the sprint from a prioritised list, the **backlog**, and agrees a goal.
- **Daily stand-up** (daily scrum): fifteen minutes to coordinate the day. What changed, what is next, what is blocked.
- **Sprint review**: show what was built to the people who asked for it, and get feedback.
- **Retrospective**: discuss how the team worked, and choose one or two things to improve.

Roles: a **product owner** decides priorities; a **Scrum master** helps the team work well; developers do the work.

## Kanban

Kanban is lighter. There are no sprints. Work flows across a **board** with columns, such as To do, In progress, Review, Done.

- **Limit work in progress**: for example, at most three items in "In progress". Finishing beats starting. Many half-done tasks deliver nothing.
- **Make flow visible**: items that wait in "Review" for days show where the team is stuck.
- **Measure lead time**: how long an item takes from start to done.

Kanban suits work that arrives unpredictably, such as support and operations. Scrum suits product teams that can plan a couple of weeks ahead. Many teams mix the two.

## What each ritual is for

| Ritual | Purpose | Sign it has become theatre |
|---|---|---|
| Stand-up | Unblock each other today | A status report to a manager, 40 minutes long |
| Planning | Agree what matters most now | Filling the sprint to 100% and never finishing |
| Estimates | Spot work that is too big or unclear | Arguing about points for an hour |
| Retrospective | Change one thing about how we work | The same complaints every time, nothing changes |
| Review or demo | Get feedback from real users | Showing slides instead of working software |

> **Rule of thumb.** If a meeting has no decision or action at the end, ask whether it is needed in that form.

## Remember

- Agile: small pieces, often; close to users; change the plan as you learn.
- Scrum: sprints, planning, stand-ups, review, retrospective, with set roles.
- Kanban: a board, limits on work in progress, and attention to flow.
- Keep each ritual for its purpose. Drop the parts that have become routine with no result.

::quiz
? A team has ten tasks "in progress" and few finished. What would Kanban suggest?
+ Limit work in progress and finish tasks before starting new ones
- Start more tasks
- Hold longer stand-ups
! Many unfinished tasks deliver no value. Work-in-progress limits push the team to finish.
? Retrospectives repeat the same complaints every two weeks. What is missing?
+ A small, concrete change that someone owns and the team checks next time
- A longer meeting
- More complaints
! A retrospective is for changing how the team works. Without an action, it is only talk.
::end
