Early in a career, an engineer is given tasks: "add this field", "fix this bug". Growth is the move from doing tasks to **owning problems**: understanding why the work matters, deciding what to do, seeing it through, and making the people around you more effective. Titles differ between companies, but this path is much the same everywhere.

## What changes as you grow

| Stage | Typical scope | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| Early | A task, well defined | How do I build this well? |
| Mid | A feature, with some unclear parts | What exactly should we build, and how? |
| Senior | A system or a project across people | What problem are we solving, and is this the right approach? |
| Staff and beyond | Several teams or the whole organisation | Which problems matter most, and how do we get many people moving on them? |

At each step, more of the work is in deciding, writing, reviewing and helping others, and less is in writing code yourself. Some people prefer to grow towards management instead; both paths need the same base.

## Habits that help at every stage

- **Finish things.** A shipped small feature beats an unfinished large one. People who reliably finish get bigger problems.
- **Understand the why.** Ask who uses what you build and what they need. Better solutions follow.
- **Own outcomes, not tickets.** If the fix shipped but the problem is still there, you are not done.
- **Make your work visible.** Short updates, clear [writing](/lesson/writing), demos. Good work that nobody knows about does not help your growth.
- **Review well**, and learn from reviews of your own work, as in [Code review](/lesson/code-review).
- **Help others.** Answer questions, pair with newer colleagues, write down what you learned.

## Keep learning on purpose

Technology changes faster than any job will teach you. Treat your knowledge like an investment:

- Learn something new regularly: a language, a part of the stack you do not know, the business domain.
- Read code and design documents from people better than you.
- Build small things on your own, where you can make every decision.
- Learn the fundamentals deeply: networks, databases, operating systems. They change slowly and explain the fast-changing tools.

## Find feedback

Ask for specific feedback from your manager and colleagues: "What is one thing I could do to have more impact?" Find a **mentor**: someone a few steps ahead who can say what they wish they had known.

> **Rule of thumb.** Look for the problem that nobody owns, that annoys everyone, and that you can solve. Solving it is often the fastest way to grow.

## Remember

- Growth means moving from tasks to owning problems, and helping others succeed.
- Finish things, understand why, own outcomes, make work visible.
- Keep learning on purpose, especially fundamentals.
- Ask for specific feedback, and find a mentor.
